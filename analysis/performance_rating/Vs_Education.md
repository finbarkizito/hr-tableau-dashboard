### High School Education Level

At the High School level, performance outcomes are concentrated toward the lower end of the rating scale. The largest share of employees fall into *Needs Improvement (34%)* and *Satisfactory (32%)*, while only *21% are rated Good* and *13% Excellent*. This distribution is typical of roles that are operational, process-driven, and often entry-level, where learning curves are steep and performance differentiation is limited by role scope rather than individual capability. From a workforce perspective, this suggests these positions function as foundational roles, requiring structured supervision, training, and clear performance management rather than aggressive performance-based differentiation.

### Bachelor’s Degree Level

At the Bachelor level, the performance profile shifts materially upward. *Needs Improvement drops to 8%*, while *Good becomes the dominant category at 50%*, supported by *30% Satisfactory* and *12% Excellent*. This indicates a strong baseline of competence across Bachelor-qualified roles, which typically form the professional core of the organisation. The dominance of the “Good” category suggests consistent, reliable performance, while the relatively small “Excellent” segment reflects natural differentiation in roles that emphasise execution and delivery over innovation or specialised expertise. Overall, this is a healthy and stable performance distribution.

### Master’s Degree Level

The Master’s level represents a clear inflection point in performance outcomes. Here, *Good (41%)* and *Excellent (35%)* together account for more than three quarters of employees, while *Needs Improvement is reduced to just 5%*. This pattern suggests that Master’s-qualified employees are deployed into roles with greater autonomy, analytical complexity, and decision-making responsibility. Performance differentiation becomes more meaningful at this level, and the organisation appears to be effectively leveraging advanced education to drive higher-quality outputs and stronger individual contribution.

### PhD Level

At the PhD level, performance outcomes are strongly skewed toward the top end. Nearly *48% of employees are rated Excellent*, with a further *34% rated Good*, and only a small minority falling into lower performance categories. This indicates that PhD holders are concentrated in highly specialised, high-impact roles where expertise, judgement, and scarcity directly translate into exceptional performance. From a long-term talent and compensation perspective, this is an optimal outcome, as the most expensive and specialised talent is delivering disproportionately high value.

### Overall Organisational Insight

Taken together, these distributions show a clear and monotonic relationship between education level and performance. As education increases, underperformance declines and excellence becomes more prevalent. This strongly suggests the company has aligned role complexity, responsibility, and expectations effectively with qualification level. From a senior analytics perspective, this is a hallmark of a well-designed talent deployment model, providing a strong empirical basis for differentiated compensation, targeted development investment, and selective retention strategies rather than broad, undifferentiated workforce interventions.
